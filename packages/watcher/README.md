# @wakeless/watcher

Status: devnet alpha, unaudited

What the public ledger shows of a run, and what an unprivileged reader can rebuild from it. The watcher is the auditor of the bindings: it reads the base layer through its own RPC reader (`getTransaction`, `jsonParsed`, read methods only, devnet asserted by genesis hash), never through a binding's word, and it loads no keypair. Private package, not published.

## The observer's view and the four checks (`observer.ts`)

For every signature: program ids, signers, plaintext token amounts (parsed transfers and token-balance deltas), plaintext recipients, Memo text, fee transfers and their recipients, as an `ObserverView` of `@wakeless/binding` plus every account key and every instruction's raw data. On each one, the four pass/fail checks of the briefing (§5):

1. no vendor address;
2. no vendor-attributable amount;
3. no Memo instruction;
4. no fee transfer naming the vendor.

The checks are mechanical. A fixed set of addresses (the vendors' wallets and token accounts, and the owner of any token account the balances credit) and a fixed set of amounts (what the owner's trajectory paid: deposits, prices, cumulative vouchers) must appear nowhere: not as a signer, a recipient, a transfer party, a memo substring, a fee transfer party, nor as 32 bytes or a little-endian `u64` inside any instruction's data. The last clause is what makes the public rail's `open` fail check 1 (its `payTo` lives in the program's arguments) and its `settle` fail check 2 (the voucher's cumulative amount sits in the Ed25519 instruction). A signature that is not on the base layer passes all four: there is nothing to read. A transaction the buyer or the escrow made is checked against the vendors; a vendor's own withdrawal names the vendor by construction (briefing §5: recipient, asset, amount; source is the vault) and is checked against the escrow and the buyer instead, as the rule for that party, not as a judgement per row. `observe(run, reader)` returns the rows and every failed check with its signature and evidence; `renderMarkdownTable` and `reportToJson` print them.

## Reconstruction (`reconstruct.ts`)

`reconstruct(transactions)` returns `{payer?, vendors, firstContactOrder, spendPerVendor, settlementTimes, edges}`: the trajectory an observer rebuilds from the signatures and nothing else. On the public rail it decodes the payment-channels program (`CHNLxYvVA28MJP9PrFuDXccuoGXAx7jBacfLEkahyGsX`) with the instruction layouts of its codama IDL (`vendor/payment-channels`, a gitignored clone of `solana-foundation/payment-channels`): `open` gives the payer, the recipients with their basis points and the deposit; `settle` and `settleAndSeal` give the voucher's cumulative amount from the Ed25519 instruction beside them and the settlement time; `distribute` gives the payout per recipient from the transfers out of the channel's token account; `topUp` moves the deposit. Direct transfers the payer signs (the `exact` scheme) count too. Order of input does not matter. Any token transfer the reader cannot attribute to a payer is returned in `edges` (owner → owner, amount, the top-level program), which is what remains readable of Wakeless: vault deposits and vault withdrawals, unlinked to each other and to any vendor relationship. Nothing is tunable; a Wakeless fixture that recovers a vendor is a failing test.

`compare(reconstructed, actual)` returns `{vendorsRecovered, vendorsActual, spendRecoveredBps, orderRecovered}`: the actual vendors the reconstruction names, the actual spend it recovers (per vendor, capped at the actual), and whether the first-contact order matches exactly.

On the three devnet signatures of `docs/public-rail-log.md` the reconstruction equals the hand-written block there (payer `94BR…TjzT`, vendor `9JD9…fPgy`, spend 100000, settlement at 1793487579). On the Wakeless signatures of the 6 Nov run it recovers no payer, no vendor, no spend and no settlement time; the two vault edges of the spike are listed as edges.

## The owner's view (`owner.ts`)

What only the buyer holds: its local trajectory (`getTrajectory()` of `@wakeless/client`, or the channel file `~/.wakeless/channels.json`), the channels with their refunds and withdrawals, the fundings it made through Aurora Confidential Intents (`fundingSource`), and readings of its private balance through the attested TEE RPC. `renderOwnerView(input)` prints every paid call (vendor, route, amount, cumulative, time), the fundings, deposits, refunds and withdrawals, the totals per vendor (calls, net to vendor, spend with fee shares, deposited, refunded, unspent in escrow), and the balance readings reconciled against the deposits, refunds and withdrawals between them. `ownerTrajectory(input)` exports the same run as the `Trajectory` reconstruct() returns: vendors in first-contact order, spend per vendor as the last cumulative of each channel (fee shares included, what a voucher on the public rail would show), and the time of every paid call. That is the `actual` side of `compare()`.

The rail exposes the current private balance only (`GET /v1/spl/private-balance`), not a history, so the balance history is the readings the owner took (`readOwnerView` adds one now), never an interpolation. A funding lands on the base layer and is not counted in the private balance. The input types are structural, so the watcher imports neither the client nor a binding; every input is parsed as untrusted data (`parseOwnerTrajectory`, `parseOwnerChannelFile`). Inside the rail the facilitator, MagicBlock's enclave and NEAR's private shard see parts of this view; the public ledger shows none of it. `test/snapshots/owner-run-1.md` is the view of run-1 (`docs/runs/run-1-trajectory.json` and the balance reading taken before it).

## The leakage indexer (`leak/`, `@wakeless/watcher/leak`)

What the ledger already knows (briefing §5b): a read-only index of the mainnet payment-channels program `CHNLxYvVA28MJP9PrFuDXccuoGXAx7jBacfLEkahyGsX`, the program the public `batch-settlement` scheme settles on. `pnpm leak:index` pages `getSignaturesForAddress` under a cap (`LEAK_MAX_SIGNATURES`, default 20000) and a since-date (`LEAK_SINCE`, optional), fetches every transaction with `getTransaction` (`jsonParsed`), decodes the program's nine instructions with the codama IDL layouts (`decode.ts`: `open`, `settle`, `topUp`, `settleAndSeal`, `requestClose`, `seal`, `distribute`, `withdrawPayer`, which is the payer's refund, `reclaim`; the voucher amount beside a settle comes from the Ed25519 instruction, the payout and refund amounts from the transfers under the instruction), and aggregates (`aggregate.ts`): channels, distinct payers, distinct payTo (the recipient with the largest share in the distribution the `open` fixes; the IDL's `payee` seat is the facilitator's sponsor key under the x402 SVM profile, counted apart as `payeeSeats`), (payer, payTo) relationships, payers with two or more distinct payTo, channels per payer (median, max), payTo by number of distinct payers (degree, top 10 published), total and median settled per channel (over the channels opened in the mint most channels use; other mints are counted, never summed), channels opened per week, instruction counts. `decodeOpen` reads the IDL's `open` layout and the earlier one without `openSlot` that the program's first months used. It picks the payer with the most distinct payTo among those with three or more and runs `reconstruct()` over that payer's signatures: the §2b argument in one picture.

This is the one mainnet path CLAUDE.md allows without the mainnet flags, and `test/leak/readonly.lint.test.ts` makes that a property of the code: the package imports nothing but types from `@solana/kit`, no signer, keypair path or `.env` reader, calls exactly `getSignaturesForAddress` and `getTransaction` (plain JSON-RPC over `fetch`, `rpc.ts`), and the script reads only its own variables. The RPC URL must name a mainnet host (`assertMainnetRpcUrl`); errors never quote it. It spends nothing and is not counted as mainnet spend.

`publish.ts` is the boundary where every address and signature is truncated to its first four and last four characters (`shorten`); the program id alone stays in full, with an explorer link. It writes `docs/leak/snapshot-YYYY-MM-DD.json` (schema `wakeless-leak-snapshot@1`, owned by `snapshot.schema.ts`, whose parser refuses a full address wherever an address goes) and `docs/LEAKAGE.md`, where every headline figure is followed by its sample size. `snapshot.schema.ts` has no imports, so the web page bundles it and reads the same `headline`. Raw responses are cached in `docs/leak/cache/transactions.jsonl` (gitignored: full addresses) so a re-render does not refetch; nothing is stored outside `docs/leak/`. x402scan has no public export or API for the `exact` scheme's per-call transfers, so that row is absent and the document says so.

## Running it

`pnpm watcher` reads `docs/watcher/public-rail.json` and `docs/watcher/wakeless-run.json` (the signatures, who made each, the owner's addresses and amounts, and the owner's actual trajectory), fetches every signature from devnet through `SOLANA_RPC_URL`, writes `docs/watcher/observer-*.json`, `docs/watcher/reconstruct-*.json` and `docs/WATCHER.md`, and exits non-zero naming the signature and the check when a Wakeless signature fails one. `pnpm e2e` runs the same four checks on every signature it emits, through the same reader, and fails on one failure. `pnpm test:devnet` fetches the run file's signatures and asserts every check passes (`test/observer.devnet.test.ts`).

Errors never quote the RPC URL (it carries an API key). The package's fixtures are real devnet responses, kept verbatim under `test/fixtures/`.
