# @wakeless/facilitator

Status: devnet alpha, unaudited

The Wakeless facilitator: the x402 V2 facilitator endpoints (`GET /supported`, `POST /verify`, `POST /settle`) and `GET /health` for the `batch-settlement-shielded` scheme, over channel and operation stores with an in-memory and a Redis implementation; the redemption worker that pays vendors through the binding and signs their receipts, with `GET /receipts/:channelId`, `POST /admin/redeem`, `GET /admin/alerts` and `GET /admin/channels`. A Hono app; the process entry serves it with `@hono/node-server` on `127.0.0.1`. What a buyer and a seller keep and send again after a `settlement_pending`, a rate limit or a lost answer is in `docs/RECOVERY.md`.

```bash
pnpm --filter @wakeless/facilitator start
```

Reads `.env` at the repo root: `SOLANA_RPC_URL` (Helius devnet), `USDC_MINT`, `ESCROW_KEYPAIR` (default `keys/escrow.json`, never under `keys/mainnet/`), `FACILITATOR_KEYPAIR` (the receipt signer, default `keys/facilitator.json`; `pnpm keys` creates it; it signs receipts and holds no funds), optional `MAGICBLOCK_TEE_RPC`, `MAGICBLOCK_PAYMENTS_API`, `REDIS_URL` (memory stores when unset), `FACILITATOR_PORT` (default 4020), `FACILITATOR_RATE_IP` and `FACILITATOR_RATE_CHANNEL` (`"<limit>/<seconds>"` or `"off"`, defaults `300/60` and `120/60`), `FACILITATOR_TRUST_PROXY` (`true` reads the first `X-Forwarded-For` entry as the caller's address; only behind a proxy you run), `FACILITATOR_LOG_LEVEL` (pino level, default `info`), `REDEEM_INTERVAL` (seconds between redemption runs, default `60` for the demo, `3600` for production, `off` for admin-triggered runs only), `FACILITATOR_ADMIN_TOKEN` (16+ characters; without it `/admin/*` is disabled), `FACILITATOR_RECEIPT_TOKENS` (`<vendor base58>=<token of 16+ chars>` entries separated by commas; `pnpm tokens` writes both into `.env` from the vendor keypairs, `--rotate` replaces them), `ATTESTATION_REPORT_PATH` (default `docs/attestation-facilitator.json`, written on every start; `docs/attestation-devnet.json` is the spike's report and keeps the raw quote). Devnet only: a mainnet RPC hostname or `MAINNET_ENABLED=true` refuses to start.

Starting builds the MagicBlock binding, which verifies TEE attestation first. If attestation fails the process still serves, so a vendor learns why instead of a refused connection: `/supported` has `kinds: []` and `extra.error: "shielded_attestation_failed"`, and `/verify`, `/settle`, `/receipts` and `/admin/*` answer 503 with that code. Nothing is settled without an attested binding, and nothing is ever simulated. With an attested binding the redemption worker reconciles every `pending` settle operation against the escrow's private balance before the first transfer (see Redemption), then runs on its interval.

## Endpoints

`GET /supported` returns the spec's `{kinds, extensions, signers}` with one kind, `{x402Version: 2, scheme: "batch-settlement-shielded", network: "solana:EtWTRABZaYq6iMfeYKouRu166VU2xqa1", extra: {escrowAddress, binding: "magicblock", teeValidator, privacyFeeBps: 10, maxIdleSecs: 604800}}`. `escrowAddress` and `teeValidator` come from the binding's attestation, not from configuration. `signers["solana:*"]` lists the receipt signer's public key, the key every receipt from `GET /receipts/:channelId` verifies against.

`POST /verify` and `POST /settle` take the standard envelope `{x402Version: 2, paymentPayload, paymentRequirements}`, with `paymentPayload.accepted` equal to `paymentRequirements`. The inner `payload` is `voucher`, `deposit` or `refund` (`docs/SCHEME.md` §4.3).

- **`/verify` reads and never writes payment state.** For a voucher it recomputes the channel id from `channelConfig` and `extra.escrowAddress`, checks the voucher names it and the buyer signed it, loads the channel and applies `nextWatermark` to the record as stored. For a deposit it validates the deposit object (signature shape, positive amount) and checks the first voucher against the claimed deposit plus any confirmed deposit, without confirming anything; `minDeposit` is not enforced by the facilitator (`docs/SCHEME.md` §1.2). A valid payload enters a short-lived verified set keyed by the payload hash for `maxTimeoutSeconds`. The response is the spec's `{isValid, payer, extra: {channelId, balance, totalClaimed, withdrawRequestedAt}}`. A verdict of `invalid_batch_settlement_svm_cumulative_exceeds_deposit` or `invalid_batch_settlement_svm_cumulative_amount_mismatch` on a known channel is corrective: its `extra` is the channel as stored with `chargedCumulativeAmount` and, once a voucher was accepted, `voucherState` (`signedMaxClaimable`, `expiresAt`, the buyer's own `signature`), for the seller to copy onto the corrective 402 (`docs/SCHEME.md` §4.2). A rejected signature or channel id carries no `extra`: the caller has not proven it is the buyer.
- **`/settle` of a voucher** requires a valid `/verify` of the same payload within `maxTimeoutSeconds` (the authorization flow); without one it fails with `shielded_verify_required`. It then moves the watermark inside the store's atomic per-channel update, running the same checks on the record as stored, and stores `latestVoucher`. The response is `{success: true, transaction: "", network, payer, extra: {commitmentId: "<channelId>:<watermark>", chargedAmount, channelState}}`.
- **`/settle` of a deposit** confirms the buyer's private transfer through the binding (`createDepositHandler`, `src/deposit.ts`). The buyer has already made it: a private ephemeral transfer from its own private balance to the escrow with `clientRefId = clientRefOf(channelId)`, built and submitted by `@wakeless/client` `deposit()`. The handler re-derives the channel id and reference from `channelConfig`, checks the record as stored (same buyer, vendor and mint; open; the voucher acceptable once the deposit counts), reserves the deposit signature as a `deposit` operation on the channel so it is credited once, then calls `binding.readDeposit(depositSignature, {clientRef, buyer, amount, transaction})`, which decodes `deposit.transaction` to check that this buyer paid the escrow exactly this amount with no Memo, that the signature landed inside the rollup with `err: null`, and that the escrow's private balance grew. On a match it records the channel with `deposit = amount` (a top-up adds to the existing deposit) and accepts the voucher inside the store's atomic update; the response is `{success: true, transaction: depositSignature, network, payer, amount, extra: {commitmentId, chargedAmount, channelState}}`. The binding answering `null` is `invalid_transaction_state` (not retryable; the operation goes back to `failed` and a later payload for the same signature is tried again); a signature already credited is `duplicate_settlement`; a confirmation of the same signature in flight is `settlement_pending` with the signature in `transaction`; a record under another buyer, vendor or mint is `invalid_batch_settlement_svm_channel_state`. A server without a handler answers 500 `unexpected_settle_error` and settles nothing. Nothing is ever simulated: the unit tests run the handler over a binding fake that answers `readDeposit` from a list of transfers the test "made"; the privacy claim is `test/deposit.devnet.test.ts`.
- **`/settle` of a `refund`** is the buyer's close request (task 8.1), sent by the buyer straight to the facilitator, never through a vendor (the vendor middleware refuses it with `invalid_batch_settlement_svm_payload_type` before asking anyone). It carries the buyer's latest voucher and a second buyer signature, `closeSignature`, over the 42-byte close message `0x56 0x02 || channelId || u64(maxClaimableAmount).le` (`@wakeless/scheme` `signCloseRequest`), so a voucher the vendor already holds can never be replayed as a close; a wrong one is `invalid_batch_settlement_svm_close_authorization`. On an open channel it moves the record to `closing` with `closingAt = now` and `refundAfter = now + extra.withdrawDelay`, adopting the voucher only when it is above the watermark (that favours the vendor alone), and answers `{success: true, transaction: "", payer, extra: {commitmentId, chargedAmount: "0", channelState: {…, withdrawRequestedAt}, closeState: {state, requestedAt, refundAfter, deposit, settled, refund}}}`. It is not kept in the settled results: the same request on a closing or closed channel answers the close as it stands (`closeState.refund` carries `{amount, signature}` once the refund has gone out) and changes nothing, which is how the client polls. `/verify` checks a `refund` the same way, writes nothing and marks nothing verified; `/settle` does not require a `/verify` for it.
- **Idempotent `/settle`.** Results are stored by payload hash (sha256 of the canonical JSON of `{paymentPayload, paymentRequirements}`, key order independent). A repeated identical payload gets the stored result with `extra.replayed: true` and moves nothing; two identical requests in flight settle once. The same voucher sent in a different envelope is not a replay and fails on the watermark as `invalid_batch_settlement_svm_cumulative_amount_mismatch`.

`GET /health` answers 200 with `{status: "ok", binding, scheme, network, uptimeSeconds}` whenever the process is up, `binding` being `ready` or `attestation_failed` (then `network` is `null`), so a vendor or a probe can tell "down" from "refusing". It names no channel, address or amount and is not cached.

`GET /receipts/:channelId` (vendor-token protected) answers `{channelId, signer, receipts: [...]}`: every facilitator-signed receipt of the channel, by period, and the signer's public key as `/supported` lists it. The bearer token names a vendor (`FACILITATOR_RECEIPT_TOKENS`); a missing or wrong token is 401 `shielded_unauthorized`, and a channel that is another vendor's, unknown or malformed is 404 either way, so a token learns nothing about other vendors' channels.

`POST /admin/redeem` runs the redemption worker once and answers its counts, `{owed, confirmed, failed, rejected, skipped}`, never an amount. `POST /admin/close` runs one close pass (below) and answers `{idle, due, settled, refunded, closed, failed, rejected, skipped}`. `GET /admin/alerts` lists the worker's alerts, newest last, each `{at, kind, channelId, opId, period, attempts, code?, apiCode?}`. `GET /admin/channels[?vendor=<base58>]` lists every channel, or one vendor's, by channel id: `{channelId, vendor, state, accepted, settled, settle: {confirmed, pending, failed, rejected, lastPeriod}}`, amounts as decimal strings of base units, and nothing about the buyer, a voucher or a signature; it is what a vendor's receipt reader cross-checks its receipts against (`pnpm vendor:receipts`, [`packages/vendors`](../vendors)). It reads the stores only, so it answers without a redemption worker or an attested binding. All three answer loopback addresses only (403 otherwise, before the token is read) and require the admin bearer token (401 `shielded_unauthorized`); without `FACILITATOR_ADMIN_TOKEN` they are disabled (403). A process without a redemption worker (no receipt signer) answers 500 on `/receipts`, `/admin/redeem` and `/admin/alerts`.

The verified set, the stored results, the rate-limit counters, the worker's backoff clock and the alerts are in memory and per process: with several facilitator processes, a vendor must reach the same one for verify and settle, and one process runs the worker, until a hardening task shares them.

## Redemption

`src/redeem.ts` (`createRedeemer`) pays vendors. Every `REDEEM_INTERVAL`, and on `POST /admin/redeem`, for each open channel with `accepted > settled` it makes one private transfer escrow → vendor through `binding.settle(vendor, amount, {clientRef, channelId, period, cumulative})` for the amount owed net of the privacy fee: `owed = accepted − settled`, `amount = owed − ⌊owed × privacyFeeBps / 10000⌋`; the fee stays in the escrow. The binding chooses split and delay; the worker never does. One transfer per channel per run; a channel paid in full is not touched again until more is accepted.

- **Operations.** The transfer is reserved first as a `settle` operation with id `settle:<period>` (periods count from 1 per channel) and payload `{period, cumulative, gross, amount, clientRef}`, so a rerun is idempotent. On confirmation the channel's `settled` moves up to the period's `cumulative` (the `accepted` at reservation; anything accepted meanwhile is the next period), the operation is `confirmed` with the transfer signature and the receipt. A thrown binding error leaves it `failed` (`attempts + 1`) and the amount owed; the worker retries it, same period and amount, after a backoff of `60 s × 2^(attempts − 1)` capped at an hour, up to 5 attempts, then stops and alerts `settle_attempts_exhausted` once. A retry goes `failed` → `pending` before the binding is called, never from `failed` directly: `pending` means a transfer may be in flight.
- **`shielded_binding_rejected`.** The rail refused the private transfer (`UNSUPPORTED_TRANSFER_ROUTE`, an ingress rejection). The operation becomes `rejected`, which is terminal, an alert `settle_rejected` is kept with the code and the rail's own code, and the worker never redeems that channel again by itself: no retry, and never a public transfer. The amount stays owed on the record for an operator.
- **Reconciliation at startup.** Before the first transfer, every `pending` settle operation (the process died inside the binding call) is settled against the escrow's private balance: what the stores account for (`Σ deposit − Σ confirmed net amounts`) less what the escrow holds is the deficit; a pending amount that fits the deficit left the escrow, so the operation is `confirmed`, the channel's `settled` advanced, and an unsigned `{reconciled: true, period, cumulative, amount}` note stored in place of a receipt (no transfer signature exists to sign over, and `GET /receipts` lists it not); one that does not fit is `failed` and retried as usual. Both are alerted (`settle_reconciled_sent`, `settle_reconciled_unsent`). The rail shows nothing of an ephemeral transfer to anyone, including the facilitator, so this is the only evidence; a buyer's deposit that landed but was not yet credited at the time of the crash can mask a sent transfer, which is why the alert names the channel for an operator to check the vendor's balance.
- **Receipts.** `signReceipt` in `@wakeless/scheme`: `{channelId, period, cumulative, amount, signature, facilitatorSignature}`, Ed25519 by the receipt signer over a 122-byte message (`0x52 0x01 || channelId || u64(period).le || u64(cumulative).le || u64(amount).le || signature`), the transfer signature inside the signed bytes. `cumulative` is gross, the channel's watermark; `amount` is what the vendor received. The receipt is stored on the operation and served by `GET /receipts/:channelId`; it is never logged, never put on chain, and never in a Memo.
- **Alerts and logs.** The worker logs `settle_pending`, `settle_confirmed`, `settle_failed`, `settle_rejected`, `redeem_run` (counts) and `reconcile` with channel and operation ids, periods, attempts and codes, never an amount or a signature (`test/logs.test.ts` holds this over a redeem cycle). Alerts are the same fields, kept in memory (the last 1000) for `GET /admin/alerts`.

Not yet here: the `settlement_pending` response of `docs/FAILURES.md` row 5.

## Close

The same worker closes channels (`closeOnce()`, run after every `runOnce()` on the timer and by `POST /admin/close`; task 8.1). A channel leaves `open` in one of two ways, and from `closing` the pass does three things in order: vendor, buyer, record.

- **The buyer's close.** The `refund` payload above. The channel is `closing`; for `extra.withdrawDelay` seconds after the request (`refundAfter`; 900 in the demo's vendors, up to 30 days) the vendor's latest voucher is still verified and settled as on an open channel, so a vendor that has served requests it has not yet presented is not cut off. After that, vouchers and deposits on the channel are refused with `invalid_batch_settlement_svm_channel_closing`.
- **Idle close.** An open channel with no activity (`lastActivityAt`) for `maxIdleSecs` (`MAX_IDLE_SECS`, default 604800, the value `/supported` advertises) is moved to `closing` with `refundAfter = now`: the vendor had that long to claim, so there is no second grace, and the same pass carries on with it. The log line is `idle close <channelId>`.
- **Due.** Once `now >= refundAfter`: (1) if `accepted > settled`, the final redemption to the vendor through the ordinary settle path (one more period, same operation, receipt and failure handling as above); a redemption that fails or is rejected stops here, so no refund ever goes out while the vendor is owed; (2) the refund of `deposit − settled` to the buyer's private balance through `binding.refund(buyer, amount, {clientRef})`, reserved first as the one `refund` operation `refund:1` with payload `{amount, clientRef}`, confirmed with the transfer's signature; a channel with nothing left for the buyer makes no transfer and no operation; (3) the record is `closed`. The refund amount is gross of the privacy fee: the fee was taken out of what the vendor received, and the buyer gets back everything it did not commit to.
- **Failures.** A refund that throws is `failed` (`attempts + 1`), retried after the same backoff as a settle, and alerted `refund_attempts_exhausted` at the cap; a refund the rail refuses (`shielded_binding_rejected`) is `rejected`, terminal, alerted `refund_rejected`, and the channel stays `closing` for an operator; nothing is ever retried publicly. Alerts for a refund carry `period: 0`. A `pending` refund at startup is reconciled against the escrow's balance like a pending settle (`refund_reconciled_sent`, with `{reconciled: true, amount}` in place of a signature and the channel marked `closed`; `refund_reconciled_unsent`, then retried).
- **Logs.** `refund_pending`, `refund_confirmed`, `refund_failed`, `refund_rejected`, `channel_closed`, `idle_close`, `close_run` (counts); ids, attempts and codes only.

`test/close.test.ts` holds the three plan rows (the grace period on a fake clock; refund equals deposit minus settled with the vendor paid first, by binding call order; idle close after `maxIdleSecs`) and the close signature, failure, reconciliation and admin-route cases. `test/close.devnet.test.ts` (`pnpm test:devnet`) deposits 2 USDC, spends 0.5 on one call, closes through `@wakeless/client`, and checks that after the grace the vendor's private balance grew by 0.5 and the buyer's by 1.4995 (1.5 less the buyer's 10 bps fee share), both as private transfers the public ledger shows nothing of; 2 Nov 2026, 21 s.

## Rate limits

Two fixed-window limits, both configurable through the environment or `FacilitatorOptions.rateLimits`, either switchable off:

- **Per caller address**, every request, checked before the body is read. The address is the connection's remote address (`@hono/node-server`), or the first `X-Forwarded-For` entry when `trustProxy` is set; a request with no address (an in-process call) is not counted. Default 300 per 60 s.
- **Per channel**, `/verify` and `/settle`, keyed by the channel id derived from the payload's `channelConfig` and the escrow, checked once the payload parses and before any check or store read. A replayed `/settle` answers from the stored result first and is not counted. Default 120 per 60 s.

Over either limit the answer is `429` with `{error, code: "shielded_rate_limited", retryable: true}` and a `Retry-After` header in seconds; nothing is parsed, checked or moved. The window starts at the first request seen for a key and the counter resets when it ends.

## Errors

Every refused request carries `{error, code, retryable}`: `code` is a scheme error code (`docs/SCHEME.md` §1.5), `error` a short message that never echoes a signature, a token, a key or a channel amount, and `retryable` is true only for `unexpected_verify_error`, `unexpected_settle_error`, `settlement_pending` and `shielded_rate_limited`.

| What | Status | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A verdict on the payment (bad signature, unknown channel, stale cumulative, closing channel, wrong payload type, deposit object malformed, settle without verify, deposit not confirmed by the binding, deposit signature reused, binding refusal) | 200 | The spec's own shape, `{isValid: false, invalidReason, invalidMessage, payer}` or `{success: false, errorReason, errorMessage, transaction: "", network, payer}`, plus `error`, `code` and `retryable` beside it. `settlement_pending` alone carries the deposit signature in `transaction` |
| A malformed envelope or requirements (not JSON, `x402Version` not 2, another scheme or network, `accepted` differs from `paymentRequirements`, another escrow, `withdrawDelay` out of range, server mode) | 400 | `{error, code, retryable}` |
| Over the per-address or per-channel rate limit | 429 | `{error, code: "shielded_rate_limited", retryable: true}`, with `Retry-After` in seconds |
| A missing or wrong bearer token on `/receipts` or `/admin/*`; an `/admin/*` request from a non-loopback address or with no admin token configured | 401 / 403 | `{error, code: "shielded_unauthorized", retryable: false}` |
| No attested binding | 503 | `{error, code: "shielded_attestation_failed", retryable: false}` |
| Unknown route | 404 | `{error, code, retryable}` |
| A facilitator fault | 500 | `{error: "internal error", code: "unexpected_verify_error" \| "unexpected_settle_error", retryable: true}` |

Stock x402 facilitator clients accept both: a 200 verdict is parsed as the spec's response, and a non-2xx body is surfaced with its status.

## Stores

Both interfaces are modelled on `ChannelStore` and `BatchOperationStore` in `@x402/svm`'s batch-settlement server, and both have an in-memory and a Redis implementation that pass one shared test suite.

```ts
const { channels, operations, close } = createMemoryStores();
// or createRedisStores(process.env.REDIS_URL, { prefix: "wakeless:" })

await channels.update(channelId, (current) => {
  const next = nextWatermark(current.accepted, voucher, amount, current.deposit);
  return next.ok ? { ...current, accepted: next.watermark, latestVoucher: voucher } : current;
});
```

- **Atomic per channel.** Memory queues writers per channel. Redis does the same inside a process and uses WATCH/MULTI between processes, each transaction on its own connection, because WATCH belongs to a connection. These connections never reconnect: a reconnect would drop the WATCH, so a lost connection fails the write instead.
- **An updater may run more than once.** On Redis it re-runs when another process wrote the channel first, so keep it free of side effects. It runs exactly once in x402's in-memory store; code written against that must not count on it here. A channel write and an operation write are two separate atomic writes, not one.
- **Operation types.** `deposit` holds a credited deposit signature (op id `deposit:<sha256 of the signature>`, so no store message names the signature; the signature itself is in the record's `signature`); `settle` is one redemption of one channel for one period (op id `settle:<period>`, the receipt in `receipt`); `claim` and `refund` are reserved for the close task.
- **Operation lifecycle.** `pending` goes to `confirmed`, `failed` or `rejected`; `failed` goes back to `pending`, and entering `failed` adds one to `attempts`. `confirmed` and `rejected` are terminal, so a rejected private transfer cannot be retried through the store. `pending` means a transfer may be in flight: a worker moves an operation to `pending` before it calls the binding, and at startup reconciles every `pending` operation against the binding before sending anything. A `failed` record is at rest, so it is the only state a retry starts from.
- **What Redis holds.** Channel amounts and voucher signatures, unencrypted, under the facilitator's own trust boundary: the facilitator is one of the three operators who see inside (with MagicBlock's enclave and NEAR's private shard). Point it at a server only the facilitator can reach. Store errors name fields and ids, never amounts or signatures.

## Logs

Structured, one JSON object per line on stdout (pino 10, `createFacilitatorLogger`). A `request` event per answered request carries `method`, `path`, `status`, the scheme `code` or the `outcome` (`valid`, `settled`, `replayed`, `receipts`, `redeemed`, `alerts`), the payload `hash` and sometimes a `channelId`; the deposit handler adds `deposit_confirmed`, `deposit_unconfirmed` and `deposit_credited_not_recorded` with the channel id and the operation id (the signature hashed); the redemption worker adds its `settle_*`, `redeem_run` and `reconcile` events (see Redemption); a 500 names only the error's class. Bearer tokens are compared, never logged. `msg` is the same words as one text line (`POST /settle 200 settled <hash> <channelId>`), which `FacilitatorOptions.log` also receives for harnesses and scripts that read lines. Never a payload field, a signature, a token, a key or an amount for a channel.

Two guards sit under that rule, neither a licence: pino `redact` censors the field names a signature, token or amount would travel under (`REDACTED_PATHS`) if an object were ever logged whole, and the destination scrubs every line of 87- and 88-character base58 strings and bearer tokens, reporting what it scrubbed in a `log_redacted` event so a test can assert the count is zero. `test/logs.test.ts` captures the stream over a full cycle (deposit confirmed through the handler, vouchers verified and settled, a replay, every refusal class, a 429, a 500, then a redeem run with one confirmed transfer and one rail rejection, the alerts and the vendor's receipts) and asserts no line carries a signature, a bearer token, a channel amount or a payload field name, and that `log_redacted` never fired.

## Tests

`test/hardening.test.ts` holds task 6.3: the idempotent `/settle`, the unknown channel, the verify-to-settle window on a fake clock, both rate limits, `GET /health`, and the rate rule parser. `test/http.test.ts` runs the app in process over a binding fake that answers only `network()` and `escrowAddress()` (nothing is transferred in a unit test; the privacy primitive is never simulated). `test/deposit.test.ts` runs `confirmDeposit` over a fake whose `readDeposit` matches only the signature, reference, buyer, amount and transaction of transfers the test recorded, and never twice. `test/request.fuzz.test.ts` fuzzes the envelope and payload parsers with fast-check. `test/deposit.devnet.test.ts` (`pnpm test:devnet`, needs `.env` and a buyer private balance of at least 0.01 USDC) makes a real private deposit through `@wakeless/client` and the real binding and settles it in process. `test/redeem.test.ts` runs the worker over a fake whose `settle` records its calls and answers a signature or throws what the test says: confirmation, failure and retry with backoff, the attempts cap, a rail rejection (terminal, alerted, the binding called once), one transfer per channel per run, the receipt verifying against the advertised signer and failing when any field is altered, startup reconciliation of a pending operation (one sent, one not; nothing resent), the loopback and token checks of the admin and receipt routes, and `GET /admin/channels` (per-channel `accepted`, `settled` and settle-operation counts, filtered by vendor, nothing about the buyer). `test/redeem.devnet.test.ts` is the definition of done on devnet: twenty paid calls to vendor1 through the real client, middleware and facilitator, then one `POST /admin/redeem` makes exactly one private transfer, vendor1's private balance grows by the owed amount minus the 0.1% fee, the receipt served under vendor1's token verifies, the transfer is not on the base layer, and the logs are clean.
